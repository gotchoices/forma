# Review — ho-bridge-2d.md

Items I disagree with or find inaccurate in
[ho-bridge-2d.md](ho-bridge-2d.md). I do not restate what the file
gets right; this list is the points to fix or qualify before any of
this lands as a metric-charge appendix.

## 1. The "two independent ladders, one per compact direction" picture
is the 2D-HO picture, not the wave-equation-on-T² picture

§"The 2D HO translation" introduces

  [a_u, a_u†] = 1,  [a_w, a_w†] = 1,  [a_u, a_w] = [a_u, a_w†] = 0

— **two** ladders, one per compact direction — and says "joint
occupation numbers (n, m) label basis states |n, m⟩ on the
tensor-product Fock space." This is the **2D simple harmonic
oscillator (2D HO)** picture: two oscillators with frequencies
ω_u, ω_w, the Fock space is the tensor product of two
single-oscillator Fock spaces, and |n, m⟩ means "n quanta in the
u-oscillator and m quanta in the w-oscillator."

The wave-equation-on-T² that
[Chapter 2 §3](../02-modes-on-a-sheet.md) actually quantises is a
different system. Its (linear) quantisation gives **one ladder per
mode** — a separate (a_{n,m}, a†_{n,m}) for every integer pair
(n, m) labelling a winding mode. The Fock space is the tensor
product over all (n, m), not over two ladders. A single-quantum
state in mode (n, m) is a†_{n,m}|0⟩, and "occupation number" in this
picture is *how many particles are in that mode*, an independent
integer per (n, m).

These two Fock spaces are structurally different objects.
Identifying "the (n, m) winding mode" with "the (n, m) joint
occupation state of two ladders" is a forced map between them, and
the map breaks down at multi-quantum states (where the 2D-HO Fock
space carries |2, 0⟩ = two quanta in the u-ladder, and the wave-
equation Fock space carries a†_{n,m}·a†_{n',m'}|0⟩ = two particles
in possibly different modes — these aren't the same vector space).

The file mostly proceeds as if the 2D-HO picture is the obvious
quantisation, but it should make the choice explicit: *which*
ladder structure is being put on top of the Ch 2 modes, and *why
that one rather than the QFT-on-T² one*. The "obvious" candidate
from Ch 2 itself is the QFT picture, and switching to the 2D HO
should be acknowledged as a substantive interpretive choice, not a
notational tidy-up.

## 2. The energy-formula caveat is good but the symmetry-algebra
claim doesn't survive the same scrutiny

§"One reconciliation worth being explicit about" honestly states:

> The HO bridge holds at the level of mode counting, ladder
> structure, and **symmetry algebras** — not at the level of energy
> formulas.

The energy half of this is correct (additive HO vs Pythagorean
KG). The **symmetry-algebra** half is overstated.

In the 2D HO (picture B), the symmetry at ω_u = ω_w is genuinely
U(2) = U(1) × SU(2), acting continuously by unitary mixing of
the two ladder operators (a_u, a_w) → U·(a_u, a_w). Degenerate
energy levels carry SU(2) irreducible representations.

In the wave equation on T² with R_u = R_w (picture A), the
spectrum is ω²_{n,m} ∝ n² + m² with (n, m) an *integer pair*. The
continuous symmetry group of this spectrum is the isometry group
of the flat torus T² × T² with equal radii — **U(1) × U(1)** of
independent translations, plus the *discrete* point-symmetry Z₄
generated by (n, m) → (m, −n). The would-be SO(2) acting on (n, m)
as continuous real coordinates does **not** preserve the integer
lattice — for any irrational angle it takes integer modes to
non-existent intermediate values — so it is **not** a symmetry of
the actual mode spectrum.

So the file's

> When the two radii are equal, the spectrum m² ∝ n² + m² is
> degenerate under any SO(2) rotation of the (n, m) plane and, more
> strongly, under arbitrary unitary mixing of the two ladders.

is only true for picture B. In picture A the available symmetries
at R_u = R_w are U(1) × U(1) × (discrete Z₄ × Z₂), not continuous
SU(2). The "real prize" of the file — SU(2) at ε = 1 — is a
feature of the 2D-HO model and does **not** transfer to the wave
equation on T² as a continuous symmetry of physical modes.

Open Question 2 already smells the problem ("What does the ε = 1
SU(2) actually generate geometrically?"). The honest answer the
file is circling is: it generates *unitary mixing of the two HO
ladders*, which is not a geometric operation on the (n, m) lattice.
The file should either downgrade the claim to "SU(2) is a symmetry
of the 2D-HO model that the bridge maps the spectrum onto,
*conditional on the bridge being valid as an interpretive
overlay*", or it should construct the SU(2) directly in picture A
(in which case it will recover only the discrete Z₄ + reflections).

This is the most consequential issue in the file; everything in
§"Symmetry payoffs (the real prize)" and most of Open Question 2
hinges on it.

## 3. Table row equating the KK gauge potential with a U(1)
generator is a category error

The translation table at lines 63–70 includes

> KK gauge potential B_μ from h_μw ([Ch 5]) ↔ Generator of the
> w-tower U(1) under dimensional reduction

A **gauge potential** B_μ is a 1-form field on extended
spacetime, taking values in the Lie algebra of the gauge group. A
**generator** of U(1) is a number — a Lie-algebra element — or an
operator implementing infinitesimal phase rotation on the wave
field. These are different mathematical objects: the gauge
potential is a *spacetime field*; the generator is a *symmetry
operator*.

The correct correspondence under dimensional reduction:

- The off-diagonal metric component h_μw → the KK gauge potential
  B_μ (a *field* on extended spacetime).
- The compact-direction momentum p_w → the conserved U(1) *charge*
  (the eigenvalue of the *generator* of w-translations).
- B_μ *couples* to that charge.

So the row should be split into two: one identifying the gauge
field, and one identifying the charge / generator. As currently
written it conflates field with symmetry operator.

## 4. "Sum of two ladder-energy contributions, *squared*" is
ambiguous and misleads

Table row (line 68):

> Rest mass m² = m_u² + m_w² (Pythagorean) ↔ Sum of two
> ladder-energy contributions, **squared**

The natural reading of "Sum, squared" is (m_u + m_w)², which is
**not** the formula. The formula is the sum of squares,
m_u² + m_w². The §"Reconciliation" paragraph clarifies this, but
the table itself, taken in isolation, is misleading. Restate as
"Pythagorean *sum of squares* of two single-direction rest masses
— structurally not the HO additive form" or similar.

## 5. (m, n) vs (n, m) ordering is used inconsistently

Within the same document:

- Line 22: m_{(m, n)} = (ℏ/c)·√((2π n/L_u)² + (2π m/L_w)²) — the
  Ch 2 convention (m, n) = (tube, ring).
- Line 37: "the (m, n) mode family is identified with topological
  winding pairs" — (m, n).
- Line 58: "Joint occupation numbers (n, m) label basis states
  |n, m⟩" — (n, m).
- Line 65: "Mode function e^{i(n u/R_u + m w/R_w)} ↔ Number
  eigenstate |n, m⟩" — (n, m).
- Line 66: "Winding pair (m, n) ↔ Joint occupation numbers
  (n, m)" — both in one row.

The flip happens silently in §"The 2D HO translation." Pick one
convention (the Ch 2 (m, n) one, since that is what the rest of
metric-charge uses) and stick with it. As written, the file
invites the reader to interpret (m, n) and (n, m) as different
labelings — which they aren't, but the inconsistency is exactly
the kind of typographic mistake that becomes a real bug if any of
this back-edits into chapter material.

## 6. The coherent-state knot upgrade is over-promised

§"Coherent-state knots" says:

> coherent states |α_u, α_w⟩ … are minimum-uncertainty Gaussian
> wavepackets on T², and trace classical trajectories without
> spreading. This is the natural formalism for talking about a
> *knot as a localized wavepacket on T²*, rather than as a plane-
> wave Fourier mode.

A 2D-HO coherent state is a **Gaussian wavepacket** centred at some
(u₀, w₀) on T² that oscillates with the HO frequencies — its
classical trajectory is an ellipse in the (u, w) plane, not a
T(m, n) torus knot. To get a wavepacket that *follows the T(m, n)
torus-knot trajectory* you need a specific superposition (e.g. a
coherent state of the (m, n)-momentum eigenmode itself, which is
not the standard 2D-HO coherent state), and the construction
depends on which classical orbit you want to follow.

So coherent states make "where the wavepacket is" well-defined,
but they do **not** automatically make "where the (m, n) knot is"
well-defined — that requires an additional step the file does not
spell out. The body text reads as if the upgrade is a clean
translation; Open Question 3 admits it isn't. The body and the
open question should be consistent: state that the coherent-state
upgrade gives wavepacket localisation but that the knot-trajectory
correspondence is the open piece.

## 7. "Now treated as kinematic" muddies Hypothesis A's derivation

§"Hypothesis A" derives the Pythagorean answer sequentially:

> treat m_u as the rest mass of a 1D-compact particle, then put it
> in motion along the (now treated as kinematic) w-direction.

"Kinematic" colloquially suggests "motional, non-compact." The
derivation works because w is **compact**: p_w is the discrete KK
momentum ℏ·2πm/L_w, and the compact-direction momentum-squared
contributes to rest mass via E²|_{p_S = 0} = (p_w c)² + (m_u c²)².
If w were genuinely non-compact ("kinematic"), p_w would be a
continuous extended-direction momentum that contributes to
*kinetic* energy in S, not to *rest* mass.

Reword "now treated as kinematic" as "now treated as a second
compact direction contributing a KK momentum" (or similar), to
prevent a reader from inferring the wrong physical setup.

## 8. Hypothesis B's headline understates the criterion

§"Hypothesis B" concludes:

> charge requires non-trivial winding in *both* compact directions.

This is the **necessary** condition. The **sufficient** condition,
which Ch 4 develops, is closure (the m | n synchronization
criterion). Single-axis modes have one winding zero — necessary
condition fails — so they don't carry charge. But two non-zero
windings that fail synchronization also don't carry charge. The
file's narration "non-trivial winding in both" captures only the
necessary half.

The body text immediately afterwards does mention Ch 4's
synchronization, so the issue is just in the section's headline
sentence. Restate as "charge requires non-trivial winding in both
compact directions **and** the closure condition that Ch 4
develops within the both-nonzero family." Otherwise the
"appendix-grade pedagogy" formulation in this section will
overshoot what the framework actually says.

## 9. The "1D SHO" identification of the u-equation, inherited from
metric-mass Ch 9, is a vocabulary remark, not a physical setup

§"The 2D HO translation" opens with:

> The u- and w-pieces of the separated wave equation are each 1D
> simple harmonic oscillators in the same sense [Ch 9] made
> explicit for the single-direction case.

Metric-mass Ch 9 §1 is careful to flag that the SHO identification
is a **vocabulary remark** — the ODE has the same shape as the
SHO equation, but the physics has no confining quadratic
potential, and the integer quantisation comes from *periodicity*,
not from oscillator-style confinement. Ch 9 §2 then proceeds to
build the ladder formalism on top of this vocabulary remark,
without claiming that the system *is* a confined harmonic
oscillator.

The ho-bridge-2d file inherits this convention but is less
careful: by promoting to "two independent ladders" and tensor-
product Fock space and continuous SU(2) at isotropy, it ends up
relying on the *physical* structure of a confining 2D oscillator
in ways the wave equation on T² does not actually have (see
items 1 and 2). The "in the same sense" hand-off to Ch 9 papers
over a real interpretive question: *is the bridge a vocabulary
remark or a physical identification?* The file slides between the
two readings as convenient. The appendix needs to pick one and
state it explicitly. If it's a vocabulary remark, then the SU(2)
"prize" gets weaker; if it's a physical identification, then the
file needs to justify why the actual wave-equation-on-T²
quantisation has been replaced by the 2D HO Fock space.

## Summary of recommended fixes

1. Make the Fock-space choice explicit: are we working in the
   QFT-on-T² picture (one ladder per mode) or the 2D-HO picture
   (two ladders for two directions)? They are different. The
   bridge as written quietly adopts the latter.
2. Downgrade the "SU(2) at ε = 1" claim to "SU(2) is a symmetry of
   the 2D-HO overlay at ω_u = ω_w; the corresponding symmetry of
   the actual wave equation on T² at R_u = R_w is U(1) × U(1)
   plus a discrete Z₄ × Z₂." Or — better — work out what the
   continuous SU(2) means geometrically in picture A *first*
   (resolve Open Question 2) before any appendix claims it.
3. Split the KK-gauge-potential row of the translation table into
   field (B_μ ↔ h_μw) and charge (p_w ↔ U(1) generator).
4. Reword the "Sum of two ladder-energy contributions, *squared*"
   row to "Pythagorean sum of squares — structurally not HO
   additive."
5. Pick one ordering — (m, n) or (n, m) — and use it everywhere.
6. Restate Hypothesis B's headline to include the m | n closure
   criterion as the sufficient condition.
7. Replace "(now treated as kinematic) w-direction" with language
   that makes the *compact* status of w explicit in Hypothesis A.
8. Reconcile the body / Open-Question-3 split on coherent-state
   knots: the upgrade gives wavepacket localisation; knot-
   trajectory tracking is a separate, open construction.
9. State up front whether the SHO identification (and the ladder
   formalism that builds on it) is a vocabulary remark or a
   physical identification. The appendix's character changes
   sharply depending on which.
