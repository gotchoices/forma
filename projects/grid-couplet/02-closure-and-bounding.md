# Chapter 2: Closure and Bounding — the Entropic Origin of the Dial

## §1. The chapter's job

Chapter 1 introduced the dial as the macroscopic node at the lattice scale: a closed periodic loop of N (point, edge) couplets in a 2π wrap. The introduction stated the dial's external behavior — bounded phase plus a discrete winding number — without deriving where that behavior comes from. This chapter carries out the derivation.

The argument is structural. Starting from the chapter 1 §7 update rule applied to two configurations of N couplets — an open 1D chain and a closed 1D loop — show how each presents itself to an external observer, and identify the asymmetry that distinguishes them. The result: closing a couplet chain into a 2π loop quantizes the chain's continuous unbounded cumulative magnitude into a discrete integer winding number, leaving a bounded phase pattern as the externally-visible state.

Two related claims fall out of the derivation:

- **The bounded-phase character of a node is derived, not posited.** Chapter 1 §2 left point values as unbounded reals; the closed-loop topology supplies the bounded interpretation as the natural reading of the closure.
- **The macro convention "node = dial at the lattice scale" follows from encapsulation, not from edge redundancy.** At the lattice scale, an external edge couples to a dial through one of its constituent points. That single coupling exposes only the connecting point's value; the dial's other points and all its internal edges are encapsulated as internal degrees of freedom.

The chapter resolves what was originally framed as a discovery target — whether edges or dials are the more fundamental object — by deriving, rather than positing, that the asymmetry is *topological* (open vs. closed) rather than *primitive* (one type vs. the other). Points and edges remain the structural primitives; dials are emergent under closure.

## §2. The two configurations

Both configurations consist of N couplets — N nodes (points) and N edges — connected sequentially. They differ only in topology.

**Open 1D couplet chain.** N points with values φ_0, φ_1, …, φ_{N−1} in ℝ; N edges, with the trailing edge (e_{N−1}) an inert stub per chapter 1 §5. The chain's two endpoints (point 0 and point N−1) are exposed: external partners may attach at either end.

**Closed 1D couplet loop.** N points with periodic identification φ_N ≡ φ_0; N edges, all active. The trailing edge (e_{N−1}) connects point N−1 back to point 0, closing the loop. No endpoints are exposed.

Figures: see [`scripts/output/configs.png`](scripts/output/configs.png), generated by [`scripts/render-configs.py`](scripts/render-configs.py).

Both configurations evolve under the chapter 1 §7 update rule. In both, the natural choice of initial conditions for a freshly-built structure is e_i(0) = 0 — the edges have no prior history to remember.

In both configurations, internal edges meet at points. The point's connection rule (chapter 1 §2) forces all edges meeting at a point to share their endpoint value with the point's value: there is *no* phase difference at the joining locations. Phase difference between an edge's tail and its head exists *along the edge's length*, between two distinct points; not at any single point.

## §3. Edges as integrated histories of point differences

The chapter 1 §7 update rule for an edge gives:

> e_edge(t+1) = e_edge(t) + (φ_tail(t+1) − φ_head(t+1))

Iterating from time 0 to t with e_i(0) = 0:

> e_i(t) = Σ_{s=1}^{t} (φ_tail,i(s) − φ_head,i(s))

Each edge's value at time t is fully determined by the history of its endpoint nodes. Edges are not independent state variables in the usual sense — they are integrated records of what their endpoint points have been doing.

This is true in both topologies. The difference between open and closed configurations does not lie in *whether* edges record point histories — they always do — but in what *macroscopic* information the topology supports.

## §4. The open chain (a foil)

An open 1D chain of N couplets, viewed externally through its two endpoints, has one macroscopic observable: the **cumulative endpoint difference**

> Δ_chain(t) ≡ φ_{N−1}(t) − φ_0(t) = Σ_{i=0}^{N−2} (φ_{i+1}(t) − φ_i(t))

External partners pinning the two endpoints set Δ_chain to whatever value they impose. The intermediate point phases evolve under the dynamics; the only externally-controllable observable is the end-to-end difference.

Δ_chain is a continuous real number, unbounded. Nothing about the chain's structure constrains it. By composition, a chain of N couplets behaves externally just like a single primitive edge would — there is no qualitatively new emergent object here, only the same continuous-magnitude character that any single edge has, scaled across a chain.

The open chain serves in this chapter as a *foil* for what closure does in the next section. It illustrates the kind of continuous unbounded macroscopic observable that closure is about to eliminate.

## §5. The closed loop — closure forces mod 2π

A closed 1D loop of N couplets has no exposed endpoints. The closure imposes the topological identification φ_N ≡ φ_0.

Two readings of this identification are available, and they give qualitatively different topologies:

- **Literal:** φ_N = φ_0 as real numbers. Under literal arithmetic, summing the differences around the loop telescopes to zero — Σ_{i=0}^{N−1} (φ_{i+1} − φ_i) = 0 identically — so only the trivial topological sector exists.

- **Mod 2π:** φ_N = φ_0 + 2π·k for integer k. Each individual difference (φ_{i+1} − φ_i) read in the principal branch (−π, π] is bounded by [−π, π], and the sum around the loop equals 2π·k.

The mod-2π reading is the richer interpretation: it admits a discrete spectrum of inequivalent topological sectors labeled by the **winding number** k — the number of complete 2π revolutions the phase makes traversing the loop once. The literal reading collapses every closed-loop configuration to k = 0, throwing away the structure the closed-loop topology offers.

A second, dynamical, argument also points to mod 2π. Consider a winding-k configuration with phases φ_i = 2π·k·i/N for i = 0, …, N−1. Apply one full clock cycle of the chapter 1 §7 update rule using literal real arithmetic. Edges acquire values from the literal differences: −2π·k/N for i < N−1, and 2π·k(N−1)/N for i = N−1 (since the closure pulls the last difference to a large positive value). On the next inhale, the resulting (e_{i−1} − e_i) terms are zero for interior points but ±2π·k for points 0 and N−1, so those two boundary points drift unboundedly under literal arithmetic. Mod 2π, the drift vanishes (2π·k mod 2π = 0) and the winding-k configuration is stationary.

So the mod-2π reading is required twice over: structurally, to capture nontrivial winding sectors; dynamically, to keep winding-k configurations bounded. The model commits to it.

The bounded-phase character of nodes is *derived* from this commitment. Chapter 1 §2 left the point's value as an unbounded real at the primitive level; the closed-loop topology supplies the bounded interpretation as the natural — and forced — reading of the closure.

The crucial structural feature: Δ_loop is *discrete* — an integer multiple of 2π. The closure operation has *quantized* what was continuous in the open chain. No continuous unbounded macroscopic observable survives on a closed loop.

The winding number is conserved under the chapter 1 §7 update rule for sufficiently small perturbations — those that do not push any individual difference (φ_{i+1} − φ_i) across the principal-branch boundary at ±π. Topology-changing perturbations large enough to cross the boundary can shift k by ±1; this corresponds to an actual phase slip, distinguishable from continuous evolution.

## §6. The dial's external view

The closed loop's residual configurational state, with the mod-2π reading in place, consists of N point phases each in [0, 2π) (a torus T^N, or T^{N−1} after gauge-fixing an overall phase reference) plus the integer winding label k.

An external edge connecting to one of the loop's constituent points — say point 0 — sees that point's phase. Bounded.

The other N−1 points and all N internal edges are *encapsulated*. They are internal degrees of freedom of the dial: they affect the connecting point's evolution through the chapter 1 §7 dynamics, but the external observer, coupled through a single point, does not see them directly.

**Winding is invisible to a single connection.** The winding number is a topological invariant of the global phase pattern around the loop — it is not stored at any one point or any one edge but in the relationship among all N points. An observer reading only the connecting point's phase cannot distinguish, instantaneously, between a k = 0 configuration and a k ≠ 0 configuration with the same phase at point 0. Detecting winding requires either reading multiple points around the loop simultaneously, or applying a perturbation designed to test the dial's response to a topology-changing event and observing whether it transitions to a different sector.

**Whether the connecting point's phase evolves in time depends on the dial's internal state.** A static winding-k configuration with the mod-2π reading is stationary at every constituent point — the connecting point's phase does not change. When the dial is disturbed (perturbations exciting the loop's discrete-wave-equation modes), the constituent points oscillate at frequencies set by the mode spectrum, and the connecting point's phase evolves. Special configurations correspond to standing-wave resonances on the loop, in which the phase pattern is stationary in time though spatially modulated. More generic configurations have phase patterns that propagate around the loop, presenting externally as a connecting-point phase with a time-varying value.

This last regime — phase rotating around the loop with a nonzero velocity — is worth flagging. It is the dial's natural way of carrying a circulating excitation that does not change the topological sector but is dynamically nontrivial. Whether such excitations couple to higher levels of the wrap-promotion ladder, and possibly underwrite an α-like leakage at higher levels, is taken up later in the project. Out of scope here, but named so the chapter 2 derivation does not foreclose on it.

The loop's externally-visible state through any one connection therefore has two parts:

- A **bounded phase** at the connecting point, possibly time-varying.
- A **discrete winding sector** k, invisible to single-point observation.

This is the **emergent dial**: a bounded-phase node with a discrete topological invariant. Larger N values give finer discretizations of the dial's perimeter (more constituent points, hence more attach locations available for external edges and a richer mode spectrum); the bounded-phase character itself does not depend on N.
## §7. The information-capacity asymmetry

Compare the macroscopic observables that each topology can support, externally.

**Open chain.** Δ_chain = φ_{N−1} − φ_0 is a continuous real, unbounded. Through the two exposed endpoints, the chain presents one continuous-unbounded variable to the outside. External-channel capacity: unbounded.

**Closed loop.** Through any single external connection: a bounded phase in [0, 2π). Capacity per connection: log₂(2π/Δφ) bits at discretization Δφ — finite. The integer winding number k is a separate topological invariant that requires multi-point observation; it carries log₂(K_max + 1) bits for a winding range bounded by K_max.

The "loss" in closure: the cumulative continuous unbounded variable Δ_chain becomes the discrete bounded integer k. In the precise Shannon sense, a real-valued unbounded variable has unbounded information capacity; an integer with bounded range has finite capacity. The continuous-information channel collapses to a discrete one.

This is the **entropy of bounding** made precise: the closure operation replaces a continuous unbounded macroscopic observable with a discrete topological invariant. The brainstorm's hunch that "bounding loses information that decompactification cannot recover" is this collapse, expressed structurally.

## §8. Encapsulation, not redundancy

A natural question, given the asymmetry just established: does closure make the dial's internal edges *redundant* with its constituent points? It is tempting to answer yes — to claim that once the loop closes, edges no longer carry meaningful information and only the points matter. The temptation is to be resisted: the closure does *not* make edges redundant in any strict sense. What it does is *encapsulate* them.

Edges remain internal degrees of freedom inside the dial. They store integrated histories of point differences that drive the connecting point's evolution under the chapter 1 §7 update. They are not redundant — perturbing an edge, holding the points fixed, generally produces different subsequent point evolution than not perturbing it. The dial has 2N internal degrees of freedom (N point phases mod 2π plus N edge values), and all of them participate in the dynamics.

What changes with closure is *external visibility*, not internal independence. In an open chain with two exposed endpoints, the edges' macroscopic effect — Δ_chain summed end-to-end — is directly visible through the endpoint pair. The edges are externally relevant because the chain has two public faces.

A closed loop has only one connecting point exposed at a time. From outside that point, the dial's other points and all its edges are hidden. The external observer sees the connecting point's phase (mod 2π); the rest of the dial's state is inside the dial, invisible to single-point observation.

So the focus shifts from edges to points not because edges become redundant, but because external observation through one connection only reveals one point's value. The dial is a compound object with internal structure that the external observer cannot directly see; the macro convention "node = dial at the lattice scale" follows from this *encapsulation*, not from any redundancy claim about edges.

Within the dial, edges keep doing their job — integrating histories, driving the dynamics, transporting information among the constituent points. They simply stop being the externally-visible information channel that they were in the open chain.

This distinction matters because some of the project's downstream analyses examine the dial's internal dynamics directly — those analyses stay live precisely because the edges are not redundant. They remain real internal degrees of freedom whose dynamics are worth analyzing.

## §9. Why the inverse fails

Constructing an open couplet chain (with its continuous unbounded macroscopic observable) from a closed dial loop (a discrete winding label plus bounded phases) would require recovering a continuous variable from a discrete one. Information cannot increase under coarse-graining; the recovery is impossible without injecting external information.

A network of closed dials connected by primitive edges *can* carry continuous magnitudes — but the connecting edges are *primitive*, not derived from the dials themselves. The dials' externally-visible state, through any single connection, presents bounded phases and a discrete winding label; neither suffices to encode a continuous unbounded variable.

This establishes the structural asymmetry: open couplet chains close into dials; dials do not open into chains. The entropy of bounding is one-directional. The brainstorm's hunch — that bounding loses information that decompactification cannot recover — is now derived from the chapter 1 §7 dynamics plus the closed-loop topology.

## §10. What this means for the project

The chapter's outcome:

- **Edge/dial primacy is settled by topology.** The point is the 0D primitive; the edge is the 1D relational object between points; the dial is emergent under closure. Neither edge nor dial is "more fundamental" — they are different structural roles distinguished by topology (open vs. closed). The brainstorm's hypothesized asymmetry survives as a topological feature, not a primitive-level one.

- **The bounded-phase character of nodes is derived, not posited.** Chapter 1 §2 left the point's value as an unbounded real. §5 of this chapter shows that the closed-loop topology forces the mod-2π reading — both structurally (only the mod-2π reading admits nontrivial winding sectors) and dynamically (only the mod-2π reading keeps winding configurations bounded). Bounded phase is what closure produces.

- **The macro convention "node = dial at the lattice scale" is justified by encapsulation.** Through any single external connection, a dial presents one connecting point's phase; the dial's other points and all its internal edges are hidden as internal degrees of freedom. The macro convention (chapter 1 §9) follows from this without claiming that edges are redundant — they remain real internal DoF, just not externally visible through one connection at a time.

- **The winding number is invisible to single-point observation.** Detecting it requires multi-point observation or specific perturbation tests. This may be relevant later for understanding how a dial's topological state couples (or doesn't) to external structure.

- **The dial's connecting point can be stationary or time-varying.** Static configurations (including pure winding-k states) present a constant phase at any one connection; non-stationary configurations present a time-varying phase, with phase-rotation-around-the-loop as a particular regime. The latter is a candidate channel for the project's later α-leakage discussion at higher wrap levels.

What the chapter does *not* establish, and is careful not to claim:

- That edges are redundant with points inside a dial. They are not. Edges remain internal degrees of freedom; closure only encapsulates them, it does not eliminate them.
- That the winding number "leaks out" or relaxes to zero. It does not. The winding is conserved under the chapter 1 §7 update for sufficiently small perturbations and is a topological invariant of the configuration.

The project's downstream chapters can take "the dial is the lattice node; its external state through any one connection is a bounded phase, and a discrete winding number labels the global topological sector" as established.
