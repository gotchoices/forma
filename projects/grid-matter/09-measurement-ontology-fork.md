# Chapter 9 — Measurement: an open ontology fork

Chapter 8 fixed the *statistics* of detection — the clicks follow |ψ|² — but not
the *ontology*: it did not say what is actually travelling between slit and screen.
Two readings survive that constraint, and this chapter's honest job is to present
the fork rather than declare a winner.

## §1 Two kinds of unknown

It helps to separate two different unknowns in a single detection. The first is the
**envelope**: which fringe a click is likely to fall in, set by the de Broglie
interference of Chapter 7. The second is the **specific draw**: exactly where,
within a small zone, a given click actually lands. Born's rule fixes the first
completely; the second — the individual outcome — is the open one. **[forma]**

The chapter deliberately leaves the *scale* of that small zone open. It is
plausibly set by the lump's own internal size (of order the Compton scale), but the
click ultimately resolves to a **detector-set** length, and asserting a specific
value here would be claiming more than is established. **[O — scale open]**

## §2 The two readings

Both of the following reproduce the Born distribution; neither is excluded by
anything in hand.

- **(i) Wave-until-interaction.** The excitation is genuinely delocalized as it
  travels, and localizes only at the moment of detection. There is no lump in
  flight — only a wave that is converted to a single click on interaction.
- **(ii) Double-solution.** There is a real, localized **lump** in flight, riding a
  delocalized **de Broglie pilot wave** that passes through both slits and guides
  it. The individual outcome is then not truly random but set by a hidden variable —
  the lump's phase or position — carried along by the pilot. This is the reading of
  de Broglie's double-solution program (and of the walking-droplet analogues).
  **[fork, open]**

## §3 Why "no collapse" is conditional

It is tempting to announce that GRID dissolves wavefunction collapse. That claim is
available **only under reading (ii)**, and even there **only if** the bulk-wave ⟷
lump **guidance dynamics** actually work out on the substrate — a construction that
is presently **owed and untested**. Under reading (i) there is still a single
outcome to account for. So "no collapse" is a conditional prospect, not a settled
result. **[O]**

## §4 The chapter's stance

The honest deliverable is therefore to **present the fork and assert neither
branch**, while naming the specific thing that would decide it: a worked
bulk-to-lump guidance dynamics on GRID. This is a question Act 2 opens, not one it
closes. **[honest]**
