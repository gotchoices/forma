# Thesis (candidate): a particle is a wave until it interacts

**Status: ONE candidate thesis, held open alongside the others** (contained-wave
/ soliton; instantiation-split). Recorded so we can pursue whatever bears fruit
without losing it. Origin: the observation that no GRID mechanism contains a wave,
and that maybe *nothing should* — free particles are meant to spread.

## The thesis

- A particle propagating freely **is a delocalized wave** — it spreads. This is
  not a failure of containment; it is what QM says a free particle does.
- **"Particleness" (localization) is relational** — an *event that occurs at an
  interaction*, not a property the particle carries between interactions.
- The wave/particle duality is then: **wave while propagating, particle at
  interaction**, where the phases of the two interacting excitations lock and the
  event localizes within the previously-probabilistic envelope.

## Why it fits GRID and eliminates (extended-space) containment

Split by dimension:
- **Extended-space position (x):** a spreading wave. **No containment needed —
  and QM agrees** (free wavefunctions spread). The seven containment negatives
  were solving a non-problem.
- **Internal structure (mass, charge = compact-mode winding):** a standing wave
  **in the compact ℵ-ring**, contained *trivially* by the ring's finiteness
  (linear KK — no focusing, no soliton). This is forma's standing-wave particle,
  correctly located in the compact sector.

So the focusing/breather apparatus becomes **largely unnecessary for free
particles** (it may still matter for *bound* states, where EM/strong forces
localize — a separate question).

## What it would explain

- **Dual slit:** the free wave passes both slits and interferes → |ψ|² fringes.
- **Single detection:** at the screen the excitation interacts with a detector;
  the **bounded substrate quantizes that interaction into one whole-quantum
  event** — one click, one node.
- **Born rule:** the event lands where amplitude/phase support it → P(x) ∝
  |ψ(x)|² — *derived*, not assumed.
- **No special observer, no measurement-chain regress:** the bound fires at
  *every* interaction, so localization is ubiquitous, not deferred to an
  "observer." Realist and causal throughout.

The bound (grid-matter's core) returns in its **correct** role: **quantizing
interactions, not containing particles.**

## The forced fork (the hard, unavoidable part)

A single spread-out quantum that yields exactly one click forces a choice — and
it is essentially *forced by* locality + single-outcome + conservation (a
Bell-type constraint):

**(A) Genuine collapse — conserved single click.** Exactly one detection, energy
conserved. **Requires nonlocality** — the spread-out wave's distant parts must be
prevented from *also* clicking. This is **mandatory, not a bug to engineer away**:
Bell's theorem forbids any *local* realist single-outcome theory. But it is
**nonlocal *correlation*, not FTL *signaling*** — QM's nonlocality obeys
no-signaling (no usable FTL communication). So the real requirement is: *nonlocal
correlations with no signaling.*
  - **GRID's candidate home for the nonlocality: the compact fiber (ℵ-line)** —
    a real channel "outside" causal space S. Collecting the distant energy /
    enforcing the single click travels through the fiber, not through S, so S
    stays causal. **The same nonlocality Bell needs.** This is GRID's potential
    *contribution*: a structural home for quantum nonlocality, rather than magic.
    Open question: does the fiber deliver correlations *without* signaling?

**(B) Reiter loading — local, multi-click allowed.** Energy deposits locally;
detectors fire on local threshold; a single quantum *could* trigger more than one.
No nonlocality, no FTL. **But contradicted by single-photon anticorrelation**
(Grangier–Aspect–Roger 1986): one photon does *not* fire two detectors. So B is
**empirically disfavored** for photons unless anticorrelation can be re-explained.

## For completeness (staying open) — two other options, neither GRID-natural

- **Many-worlds:** no collapse; all outcomes branch. Avoids both horns but a
  firing bound on GRID *is* a definite local event (a collapse), so many-worlds
  sits awkwardly here and still must explain the observed single click
  (branch-relative).
- **Transactional / retrocausal:** an emitter–absorber handshake localizes via
  advanced+retarded waves. Nonlocal-in-time; **contradicts GRID's forward-causal
  discrete update rules** unless GRID is given advanced-wave structure.

## Bottom line

For a realist, forward-causal, local lattice like GRID, the honest options collapse
to essentially **(A) with the nonlocality carried by the compact fiber** — you
cannot avoid the nonlocality (Bell), but GRID can give it a *home* and must show it
yields correlations without signaling. **(B)** is disfavored by anticorrelation.
The decisive, simulable test: do two spread-out packets interacting on the bounded
lattice localize to **single events distributed as |ψ|²**, and can the fiber carry
the enforcement without signaling?
