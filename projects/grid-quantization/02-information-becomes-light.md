## Outline

- **2.1 The restoring term.** −1/3 reflection = sign-flipped residue = a
  "spring": a static edge excitation cannot stay static.
- **2.2 Oscillation + propagation.** The junction rule as the discrete
  wave equation; travelling waves emerge.
- **2.3 Dispersion.** Measured linear ω ≈ 0.41·k; non-dispersive at long
  wavelength.
- **2.4 E, B, and the two polarizations.** The Y-junction's 3-fold
  symmetry gives a 2-D transverse mode space (the −1 eigenspace of the
  scatter matrix). **Spin / helicity comes from the *phase* — the clock
  face (A3) — not the restoring term:** the helical eigenmodes
  (1, ω, ω²), (1, ω², ω) = E ± iB are *phase progressions* around the
  junction, so a definite handedness requires the edges to carry
  relative phase (classical complex *phasors*; real Tier-1 amplitudes
  give only linear polarization). The restoring term drives oscillation
  but is **silent about handedness**. This phase is *classical*
  (circular polarization is ordinary EM) — distinct from the *quantum*
  complex amplitude ch. 5 needs for occupation. No ℵ-line.
- **2.5 What "light" *is* here.** A propagating edge-wave excitation
  carrying coupled E, B; its handedness (spin) is set by the junction
  phase, its propagation by the restoring term.
