# Chapter 8: Where α appears

## §1. The chapter's job

The fine-structure constant α ≈ 1/137 is the dimensionless coupling that characterises the strength of electromagnetic interaction. It has no derivation from first principles in standard physics; its value is taken as a measured input. The wrap-promotion ladder of chapter 7 places charge at L3 (the 2-torus closure with U(1) × U(1) topological windings); α, as a property *of* charge's interaction with itself, is a candidate L3 quantity.

This chapter does two things and explicitly does not do a third.

It does locate α on the ladder structurally — α plausibly first becomes available at L3 for a structural reason parallel to mass first becoming available at L2 (chapter 7 §4.1). The argument is in §2 and §3.

It does articulate a *wrap-of-a-wrap* framing — α may live at L3 because L3 is the first rung where the substrate has been wrapped *twice*, not because of any specific lattice geometry. The framing is in §4. It has consequences for where α's value should and should not be searched for.

It does *not* derive α = 1/137. Substantial prior effort on geometric-ratio searches across many lattice constructions has not produced 1/137 in any natural way, so this chapter does not propose a list of geometric ratios to test. The honest scope (§7) is that α's structural location is identified; its specific numerical value is open work that probably needs a different toolkit than lattice geometry alone.

## §2. The mass-at-L2 parallel

Chapter 7 §4.1 established that mass is *not available at all* on a 1D substrate. The structural chain was: mass requires v_g = 0 at a band extremum; band extrema require coord ≥ 3; coord ≥ 3 requires the substrate to be at least 2D. L1 is 1D, so L1 has no mass. The L1 → L2 wrap raises the substrate to 2D, unlocking the band-extremum phenomenology. Mass is the simplest new conserved quantity that the L2 topology supports.

The parallel claim for α: α is *not available at all* below L3. The structural chain is:

- α is the *coupling between charge and the gauge field that mediates EM interaction*. As a coupling, it requires two distinct topological structures to ratio against each other.
- Two distinct topological invariants on the substrate require *two independent winding directions*, i.e. π₁ of rank ≥ 2.
- π₁ of rank 2 first appears at L3, the 2-torus T² with π₁ = ℤ². Below L3 there is at most one winding direction (the L1 ring's single winding, which L2 retains as the embedded loop's winding).

Linking the chain: α → cross-coupling → two windings → π₁ rank ≥ 2 → T² (= L3). Below L3 there is no cross-coupling structure; α has no structure to be defined as. At L3 the cross-coupling structure first appears; α first becomes definable.

This is not a coincidence with mass-at-L2. The wrap-promotion ladder is structurally: each rung makes available the simplest new conserved quantity that the new topology supports. Light is the simplest L1 quantity; mass is the simplest L2 quantity; charge is the simplest L3 quantity; α is the simplest L3 *cross-coupling* — and the lowest rung where any cross-coupling is structurally definable. The pattern matches because the ladder is what it is.

## §3. Why α plausibly first appears at L3

Three independent structural arguments. Each is partial; together they make the L3 location nearly forced.

### §3.1 Cross-coupling needs two topological invariants

A coupling is a ratio between two structures. α's standard reading is the strength of EM interaction expressed as a dimensionless number — equivalently, the ratio of the electromagnetic interaction energy to a natural energy scale set by ℏc and the elementary charge. For α to have meaning as a ratio, the numerator and denominator must be distinguishable: the *charge* (one topological structure) and the *coupling field* through which charges interact (a second structure that lives on cycles).

Below L3 the substrate has at most one type of topological invariant — winding in one direction. There is no "second structure" for charge to couple to via a non-trivial ratio. α as a ratio collapses (numerator equals denominator); the dimensionless number is undefined.

At L3 the U(1) × U(1) structure provides exactly the second invariant. The two windings (w_α, w_β) are independent and dual. α is the natural cross-coupling between them.

### §3.2 The U(1) × U(1) duality structure

The two U(1) factors of L3 are related by Hodge duality: in (2+1)-dimensional electromagnetism on T², the electric and magnetic U(1) charges (∮ E · dl and ∮ B · dl, both quantised in units of 2π) are interchanged under the duality transformation. This is the discrete-lattice version of electric-magnetic duality.

α has a natural reading in this picture. The duality is exact at α = 1, where the two charges interact symmetrically. At α ≠ 1, there is an asymmetry between the electric and magnetic sectors, and α measures the strength of the asymmetry. The structural existence of the asymmetry — and hence of α as a non-trivial number — requires both U(1)s to be present, i.e. requires L3.

### §3.3 Quantised charge enables a dimensionless ratio

α is dimensionless. Its dimensionlessness depends critically on the quantisation of charge: e is integer-valued in units of the elementary charge, ℏ and c set natural energy and time scales, and α = e²/(4πε₀ℏc) is independent of the unit system because of the integer-quantisation of e.

Below L3 there is at most a continuous-valued plaquette flux φ (chapter 7 §4.3). Without integer quantisation, "α" as a ratio depends on how flux is normalised — the would-be coupling rescales arbitrarily with conventions and has no fixed dimensionless value. At L3 charge becomes integer-quantised by topology, and α becomes a definite dimensionless number.

The three arguments converge: cross-coupling needs two invariants, the U(1) × U(1) provides them with a duality structure where α has a natural reading, and L3's integer-quantisation of charge is what makes α a definite dimensionless number rather than a unit-dependent placeholder. All three first hold at L3, which is why α first becomes available there.

## §4. The wrap-of-a-wrap framing

A reading worth taking seriously: α appears at L3 because L3 is the first rung where the substrate has been *wrapped twice*. L1 wraps the substrate once (closes it into a loop). L2 embeds the loop in a 2D region (no new wrap, just embedding). L3 wraps the second direction (a wrap on top of the existing wrap). The wrap operation is the same operation each time, but the *number of times it has been applied* changes — and α is the simplest quantity that depends on this iteration count being ≥ 2.

### §4.1 Wrap depth as the relevant variable

This framing makes wrap *depth* the primary structural variable, not lattice geometry. Light (L1) is what one wrap produces. Mass (L2) is what one wrap *plus a 2D embedding* produces — same wrap depth, different embedding. Charge (L3) is what *two wraps* produce. α, as a coupling between two charges, is a property of the two-wrap structure as a category — independent of the specific lattice's edge directions, cell counts, or coord values.

The empirical evidence is consistent with this: α has the same value (1/137) regardless of which physical lattice one chooses for the discrete substrate, regardless of cell spacing, regardless of cosmological scale at which we measure. If α were a geometric ratio of specific lattice constants, it would presumably depend on which lattice we picked. That it does not depend on these choices is consistent with α being a property of the *wrap depth*, not the lattice's specific geometry.

### §4.2 What this implies for searches

Searches for α as a ratio of geometric constants — packing densities, edge-length ratios, cell counts per unit volume — are structurally pointing at the wrong layer. The wrap-of-a-wrap reading suggests α is a *categorical* invariant of the second-wrap operation, not a *geometric* invariant of the lattice it acts on. The candidates worth investigating are accordingly different in kind:

- *Topological invariants of the wrap operation itself.* Chern numbers, Wess-Zumino-Witten levels, anomaly coefficients — quantities that classify second-wrap structures up to homotopy without referring to specific lattice geometry.
- *Coupling-strength fixed points.* α is the IR fixed point of the QED renormalisation flow. The fixed point is set by the *structure* of the flow (anomaly cancellation, gauge invariance, fermion content), not by where one starts. If the wrap-of-a-wrap structure dictates the RG flow's fixed point, α emerges from the fixed-point structure rather than from lattice constants.
- *Categorical invariants.* In topological field theory, certain numerical constants emerge from the categorical structure of the underlying fusion category (e.g., quantum dimensions, twist factors, F- and R-symbols). α might be such an invariant of the wrap-twice category, expressible only in those terms.

These candidates are honest hypotheses; this chapter does not develop any of them computationally. What §4 establishes is that they are the *right kind* of candidate, not the geometric-ratio candidates one might naively try first.

## §5. Leakage as a structural reading

A hypothesis worth flagging, even though the chapter does not commit to it: each wrap may have an associated *leakage* — a conserved quantity that emerges as the residue of the wrap operation on the level below. The wrap is the verb; the leakage is what falls out as a noun on the next rung.

### §5.1 Leakage by rung

Tentative identifications:

- *L0 → L1 leaks light.* The L0 substrate has local dynamics with no global propagation. The L0 → L1 wrap (extend & close) leaks the propagating wave: information that was confined to local cells now flows around the closed substrate. Light is the leakage.
- *L1 → L2 leaks mass (or possibly entropy).* The L1 ring carries free-propagating waves with no localised energy. The L1 → L2 wrap (embed in 2D, bound the loop) leaks rest mass — energy that is now localised and stationary, no longer propagating. The "mass leak" reading also has a thermodynamic flavour: a closed bounded region with non-zero rest energy is a localisable thermodynamic system, and entropy may be the relevant leakage observable rather than (or alongside) mass.
- *L2 → L3 leaks charge.* The L2 plaquette has a continuous-valued flux φ. The L2 → L3 wrap (close the second direction) leaks integer-quantised charge: φ becomes one of the two windings (w_α, w_β), now restricted to ℤ rather than ℝ. The wrap forces quantisation; the integer winding is the leakage.
- *L3 → L4 leaks ?* Following the pattern, the third wrap would leak a third conserved quantity — the candidates from §6 of chapter 7 (a third U(1) charge, spin, generation, cosmological invariant).

### §5.2 Why this framing matters for α

If leakage is the structural mechanism, α is naturally read as the *leakage rate* at the L2 → L3 wrap rather than as a geometric constant of the lattice. The rate at which integer-quantised charge "leaks out" of a plaquette flux as the second direction wraps is what α would measure. This is a coupling-constant interpretation that is independent of lattice geometry and consistent with §4's wrap-of-a-wrap framing.

The chapter does not claim this is correct. It articulates the framing because it is structurally suggestive: a hypothesis worth keeping on the table while the lattice-gauge-theory machinery to test it is not yet built.

A complementary thought worth recording: if leakage is universal in wrapping, the L1 → L2 leakage is plausibly *entropy* at the appropriate level of description. Mass and entropy are both localisation-related observables; mass measures the localised energy and entropy measures the configurational ignorance of how that localisation was achieved. The wrap forces both. Identifying which is the *primary* leakage — and which is derived — is open work that the chapter does not resolve.

## §6. The L4 connection (speculative)

If α requires only two windings, L3 is sufficient. If α requires three or more windings — for instance, because cross-couplings among multiple gauge sectors are needed to fix its specific value — then L4's third winding is part of the structure. Chapter 7 §6 identified candidates for the third winding (a third gauge U(1), spin, generation, a cosmologically scaled invariant); each maps onto a different potential origin for α's value.

A specific question worth flagging: *is the universe a 3-torus or a 4-torus?* The 3-torus reading takes only the three spatial dimensions as wrapped. The 4-torus reading wraps time as well. If time is itself a wrap dimension at the cosmological scale, then L4 is a different rung — one that includes a temporal wrap — and α's location may fold in this temporal structure. This connects to standard cosmological discussions of closed timelike curves, periodic time models, and the de Sitter / anti-de Sitter classification of compact-spacetime cosmologies.

The chapter notes the question and does not answer it. The answer requires data and modelling outside the project's scope.

### §6.1 Renormalisation flow as the third axis

The most physically motivated reading of L4 for α-purposes: α(μ) runs with energy scale under the renormalisation group, with α(μ_atomic) ≈ 1/137 and α(μ_Z) ≈ 1/127. If the third winding direction at L4 corresponds to the energy-scale axis (μ as a third lattice coordinate), then α has a value at every μ, and the IR fixed point that gives 1/137 is fixed by the L4 topology. This makes α a function of where one is on the third axis — a structurally L4 quantity rather than an L3 constant.

This interpretation does not require time itself to be wrapped; the energy scale is a derived parameter, distinct from time. But it does require L4 to be physically meaningful in a way that goes beyond cosmological speculation. The chapter flags this as a candidate worth developing if the wrap-promotion framework is to extend cleanly to RG flow.

## §7. Honest scope

The chapter has done the following:

1. Located α on the wrap-promotion ladder: L3 (with possible L4 contributions through RG flow or cosmological structure).
2. Articulated the structural reason α first becomes available at L3: cross-coupling needs two topological invariants, U(1) × U(1) provides them with a duality structure, and integer-quantised charge makes α dimensionless.
3. Argued that α is plausibly a categorical invariant of the wrap-of-a-wrap operation rather than a geometric ratio of lattice constants. This makes geometric-ratio searches the wrong kind of search and points the next move at topological / categorical / RG-fixed-point candidates instead.
4. Flagged a leakage framing that may or may not be correct: each wrap leaks the simplest new conserved observable on the next rung, and α may be the leakage rate at the L2 → L3 wrap.
5. Identified the L4 questions, including whether time itself is a wrap dimension and whether RG energy scale is the third axis.

The chapter has not done the following:

- Derived α = 1/137 from any candidate.
- Identified which specific topological / categorical / RG-fixed-point construction gives α.
- Resolved whether α requires only L3 or genuinely needs L4 to acquire its specific value.
- Settled whether the leakage framing is the correct way to read the wrap-promotion ladder.

What would be needed for a derivation: the specific lattice-gauge-theory of charge at L3, computed on the chosen substrate (hex in 2D, diamond in 3D), with the categorical invariants of the second wrap calculated explicitly. If one of those calculations lands near 1/137, the chapter has a numerical result; if none does, the structural location is still real but the value remains an empirical input. Either outcome is honest.

## §8. Closing pointer

α's structural home on the wrap-promotion ladder is L3, with possible L4 contributions through RG flow or a third winding. Its specific value is open: the chapter argues that geometric-ratio searches are the wrong kind of search, and the right kind — categorical invariants of the second wrap, RG fixed points — has not been computed in this project.

The wrap-promotion ladder ends at L3 (with the L4 thread acknowledged as speculative). The next chapter consolidates the project's results.
