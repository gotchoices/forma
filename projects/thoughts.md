# More thoughts about GRID, Gravity, EM

## General
Several projects have been undertaken to try to refine the GRID model and/or explain, in a more detailed way how physics (mass, charge, QM, etc) emerges from it.  Some progress has been made, but there is more to be done.

- Most recently we completed GRID-matter.  What did it accomplish that is novel?  And what is left undone?
- Modern gravity models (Jacobson, Verlinde) are said to treat gravity as an entropic approach.  I've been struggling to understand better what that means.
- In Forma, rather than defining fields and resulting forces (SM), we try to define preferred (lower) energy states.  Mass/charge emerge from standing waves of light (information in motion) in a lattice, on one or more compact dimensions.

## Entropic Model
- It seems enough, as an explaining "mechanism" to show that these waves of energy will "seek" a low energy state, or to maximize entropy.  Have I got that right?  Is it stated correctly and accurately?
- If correct, it seems like I only need to show a few things:
  - The compact dimensions (tori) are like more complex versions of the single compact ring in metric mass.
  - Space/S is a more complex version of the single linear dimension.
  - So particles are standing waves in a Ma tori at some location along an S cylinder (or a cylinder whose length is along S and tube is an Ma torus).
  - If two such mass particles at a distance are "more organized" or "require more information to explain" than if they were closer together, then it seems like they would experience a migration toward each other.  This is part of a gravity mechanism.
  - If this effect can be shown to fall off at the correct rate, I think that's the main "other part" that is needed.
  - We already showed that a mass (1D) standing wave doesn't have a clear direction on the compact dimension.  If the two standing waves are at a lower energy state (higer entropy) when together than apart, I can imagine them "beating together" and overcoming marginal losses (i.e being more efficient together than apart).
  - Now to charge:
  - Metric-charge showed that charge is a "higher order" form of mass where the standing wave is 2D and so produces both mass _and_ charge (analogous to Kaluza/Klein).
  - Unlike mass, this is directional or polarized.  It can be spin up or spin down.  It can also be positive or negative.
  - I can imagine a low entropy state of two defined particles at a distance that are opposite.  What higher entropy might they accomplish than to annihalate each other (in the case of particle/antiparticle) or get as close as possible to each other (in the cases like proton/electron).  Joining would erase all their information.  Colocating would likely minimize it.  Seems like an attractive "force".
  - And what is the low entropy opposite?  Two like charges being apart and distinct--to some degree, the more the better (lower entropy).
  - How could we show that it is 1/r^2?  Then we would have it.
  - Now to "matter"
  - If both gravity and EM are simply entropy in action, then we have two forces:
  - One is small, long reaching and always attractive because the mass winding would find it more efficient to join (aggregate).
  - The second is stronger at proximity but not long reaching.  It would attempt to anahilate (join) if it could.
  - Finally, we have the quantization of the grid.  Only one quantum of energy can exist in a single mode space at a time.
  - Atoms form naturally because of the complementary charges of the electron sheet and quark sheet (proton/neutron).
  - Atoms looks like small + core wrapped in a much larger (when viewed from a _compton_ or electromagnetic scale rather than gravitational viewpoint) - envelope.
  - So massive particles come together naturally to form aggregated matter
  - But they can only get so close because the electron charge is dominant at the atomic boundary (electron cloud/surface)
  - Matter finds equilibrium when the atoms are close enough to satisfy gravity and far enough to keep the electrons away from each other.
  - In addition, there are bonding mechanisms where the atoms "share" electrons (a mechanism we won't delve into deeper here).

## Time Dilation Model
Another possible explanation for gravitational and EM forces might be found in time dynamics.  Under GRID, there is an assumed "clock" that cycles the nodes and edges to perform their scattering functions.  In most cases, we have assumed some sort of "master clock".  But we also entertained the idea that nodes/edges are "self-clocking" or at least "independently clocked".
- Imagine a simplified Ma-Space-Time with only x, c, t dimensions.
- A mass can be created by a standing wave in c.
- This demonstrably produces a massive (inertial) particle in S(x)
- What if, on the grid, independent grid clocks are affected by the standing wave (i.e. mass) in such a way as to slow down slightly
- Now, along the x axis, we have spots where progress is slower than others.
- In a region of space along x where there is a gradient (variation) of clock speed (time), the progression of the matter in that location would be skewed to one side or the other (+x or -x), bending toward the area of greater mass.
- As x progressed to a later time slice (in t), this could produce a motion (or falling) in a particular direction.  This is could be much of what is needed to explain a gravitational field.
- Metric-mass proposes that a massive particle has no particular direction but is, in a sense, majorana.  But a charge (metric-charge) is the next higher order and has polarity + and -.
- Perhaps charge can also affect time (GRID clock rates) in a similar manner but in such a way that like particles repel and opposites attract.  This would be an interesting generalization of EM and gravity.
- We would need a way to explain why EM can be shielded and gravity can not.
- We would also need a specific mechanism showing how the time dilation effect occurs.
