On GRID Scattering:
- I've been a little uncomfortable with the idea of a uniform scattering on grid nodes because it seems like a particle can't stay coherent
- In the 2D cylinder model of metric-mass, I imagine a particle as a circulating wave around c but which stays local/coherent in x
- It should be able to move in x, but it seemed like it should be able to stay together somehow as it moves (or stays still)
- I think we've shown that the uniform scatter function _does_ dissapate the wave out in x, correct?
- I proposed the idea of saturation/overflow as a way of explaining how a particle could stay _together_ in x but still circulate in c
- I'm starting to wonder if the particle doesn't have to actually stay _together_ in order to still be _coherent_
- IOW, could a particle spread out through x and still have a locus and a character due to its standing-wave nature in c?

On The Sinc Function
- I have raised the sinc function as one possible example of how a wave could have a locus of energy and yet still spread out a diminishing influence theoretically to infinity
- I think we disproved that sinc specifically works for our needs
- But is it possible that it provides an analogy for something that _would_ work?

Observed vs Observer
- The two slit experiment (at least originally) seemed to posit some objective observer and an objective particle/wave being observed.  The mysterious nature of the results seems to imply that the observer is not so objective after all and is, in fact, a part of the experiment.
- Forma posits that matter is made of light
- In the two slit experiment, we send light through the slits (at least in the photon version) and "observe" how it interacts with matter (the screen).
- In the forma view, the screen is also light of a form as are the barriers that define the slits.
- So we are really talking about light interacting with light
- The primary difference is that matter (Ma) is light oscillating in a more complex way because it involves standing waves on one or more compact dimensions.

The Posit
- So what if we embrace the idea that a particle spreads in x
- But can we determine if it can both spread _and_ stay coherent?  Presumably, this means that it retains distinct Ma properties, including a locus.
- Is there a way the wave can spread/propagate in x that does not diminish its coherence?
- Is there a way a wave can spread/propagate in x that does not diminish the energy it contains at its locus?
- I'm imagining something that is _like_ a sinc in that it has a center (locus) but it also has a wave pattern extending off into infinity
- Now imagine our interaction again between observer particle (light on x,c) and observed photon (light on x)
- Imagine further that the wave function of both photon and particle are extending off into space by some definable function that fills all space (i.e. a field)
- At some point, they come into contact.  The loci are now the same.
- At this moment of interaction, we have two wave functions overlapping who share certain qualities.
- Is there a way that they could suddenly sum to zero everywhere in space (x) while interfering constructively at/near the common locus?
- This would explain the "snap" of observation
- But as a much more significant bonus, it explains the "collapse" of the wave function everywhere else
- The photon truly did go through both slits.  Its wave function exists everywhere, including both slits as well as the space on both sides of the barrier.
- What is unique to this posit (I think) is that the observer's wave function also exists everywhere.
- This is the key to collapse.  It is when the two waves come into alignment that interference creates a collision _event_.

Followups
- If this posit holds, there are three types of interactions
  - p-p (photon-photonn)
  - p-Ma (photon-particle)
  - Ma-Ma (particle-particle)
- For p-p, in most cases, superimposition allows photons to ignore each other.  But there are a few rare cases where they do interact.  Pair production is one of them.  I think we have at least one viable candidate for this in a grid non-linearity, including a saturation model.
- For p-Ma, we have a new (is it really novel) way to look at the two-slit experiment, hidden-variable theory, and probability collapse.
- For Ma-Ma, perhaps we have a new way to understand gravitational attraction and charge interaction.  Specifically, if massive and/or charged particles exist everywhere as waves, not just at their locus, we now have a physical explanation for field.  If their waves can interfere in just a certain way, we could likely explain attraction and repulsion.
- As a thought experiment, imagine two massive particles (i.e. standing waves on c, spread out in x).  They are in reach of each others' fields (their wave functions overlap).  What if the waves between them interfere additively and the waves elsewhere interfere subtractively (somehow).  Could this have the effect of making each particle's locus "a little bit more concentrated" in the space between and "a little less so" elsewhere?  IOW, the loci of both particles would begin to migrate toward each other in time.  Gravity.
- Charged particles have an additional degree of freedom (2D compact dimensions) and so they are polarized.  Now the two particles' waves may interact attractively if they are opposite charges or repulsively if alike.

Goal
- If this is a viable model, we would have several things to investigate
- Does our existing GRID scattering function produce this behavior (i.e. does a particle exist everywhere and does it maintain its Ma properties still)?
- If not, does such a wave function exist for particles (and photons) that they can be made to interact in the 3 ways mentioned above?
- If so, can we conceive of a GRID node function that _does_ produce the desired wave functions?
