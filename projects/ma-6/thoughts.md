# Some more brainstorming

- Under this framework, I'm guessing that there is no such thing as a specific ring and tube relationship
- More likely that the smaller dimension serves as tube and the larger as ring.  A single dimension might be ring for one particle and tube for another.
- We probably need a different nomenclature
- It was a little unfortunate that our studies' mode numbering evolved as (e_r,e_t,v_r,v_t,p_r,p_t)
- I would like to be able to write an 11 degree metric in a form where the 4 spacetime components are easily recognized and the other 7 are in size order
- Some of our later studies started with aleph and end with time.  That makes spacetime read backwards from some standards but I like that it reads from top to bottom as Material-Space-Time.
- What would be a good mode nomenclature?  Should we go to different brackets to denote the new standard?  For example {aleph, m1p, m2p, m3e, m4e, m5v, m6v, Sx, Sy, Sz, t}.  That doesn't make explicit that p and e share a dimension but it might be fine.
- Smallest dimension is always listed first.
- Is it reasonable to assume that the smallest dimension acts as tube for closure purposes?  If we get a turn there, we evaluate the other dimension to act as the mass carrier and look for closure.
- Is it reasonable to assume that it is cheaper to cross a plane than to go diagonal in 3D?  If so, that can be our sheet constraining rule for now.
