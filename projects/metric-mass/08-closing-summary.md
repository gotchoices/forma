# Chapter 8 — Closing summary

**Status:** Bare outline. To be filled out as prose once
Chapter 7 lands.

The project asked a narrow question
([Chapter 1](01-foundation.md)): if we put only light on a
manifold with one extended spatial dimension and one compact
extra dimension, with a bare diagonal metric, what does the
math say? In particular, does anything resembling mass
emerge, and what does the metric have to look like to support
it?

This chapter consolidates what was established along the way,
what was ruled out, what remains open, and what the project
sets up for follow-up work. It introduces no new derivation —
its job is to make the project's results legible as a whole.

## Bare outline

### 1. What the project asked

Restate Chapter 1's question in clean form: starting from
nothing but light on a minimal (t, S, u) manifold with a bare
diagonal Lorentzian metric, what does □φ = 0 produce, and what
does the metric have to do to remain consistent with the
solutions?

Two specific sub-questions: (a) does anything mass-like
emerge? (b) does the bare metric stay diagonal, or are
off-diagonals forced?

### 2. What was derived

The project's positive results, in the order they were
established:

- *A discrete mass spectrum* (Chapter 2). The wave equation on
  M produces a separable family of modes labeled by integer n
  and continuous k_S, with rest mass m_n = ℏ|n|/(R_u c). Mass
  is forced by the periodicity boundary condition in u; it is
  not assumed.
- *Inertial behavior* (Chapter 2 §6). The candidate mass m_n
  satisfies p_S = m_n · v_g in the slow-motion limit — the
  operational definition of inertial mass.
- *Geometric distinction between light and mass* (Chapter 3).
  Light is the n = 0 mode (no u-structure); mass is the n ≠ 0
  family (helical phase contour around u, but straight
  worldline). The wave-realist reading of φ makes mass a
  literal wave with internal compact-direction winding.
- *Mode-interaction predictions* (Chapter 4). In linear theory,
  ±n modes pass through each other; the static superposition
  has rest energy 2 m_n c²; superposition cancels n-linear
  off-diagonal stress-energy entries.
- *Off-diagonal sourcing pattern* (Chapter 5). Mass at rest
  sources g_tu under Einstein's equations; mass moving sources
  g_tu, g_tS, g_Su. The bare diagonal metric is *not*
  self-consistent under Einstein's equations whenever a single
  massive mode is present.
- *Gravitational coupling* (Chapter 6). Bending of spacetime
  is set by total energy density; ±n superposition doubles
  rest energy and therefore doubles bending.
- *Mechanism for ±n biasing* (Chapter 7). Off-diagonal g_Su
  shear lifts the ±n degeneracy for moving modes. A thermal
  ensemble in a sheared region equilibrates with asymmetric
  ±n densities.

### 3. What was ruled out

- *Inertia shielding by ±n superposition.* Energy adds linearly,
  not cancels (Ch4 §6, Ch5 §7).
- *Gravitational shielding by ±n superposition.* Same reason
  (Ch6).
- *Single-mode rest-mass dependence on the sign of n.* Mass
  depends on n², so m_+n = m_−n (Ch3 §3, formalized in Ch5 §3
  via the structural matching).
- *±n distinction as observable on a fully diagonal manifold.*
  Within the bare metric, ±n is a wave-mechanical handedness
  with no physical observable that distinguishes it (Ch3 §3,
  Ch7 §3).

### 4. What was unexpectedly found

Three points worth flagging as findings the project did not
anticipate at framing:

- *Mass at rest sources off-diagonal metric components*
  (Ch5 §5). The Einstein-equations consistency analysis
  required g_tu to develop in the presence of a single massive
  mode. This is a Kaluza-Klein-style coupling that emerges
  even in our minimal mass-only setup.
- *±n superposition cancels off-diagonal sources but doubles
  diagonal* (Ch5 §7). The cancellation pattern is partial in
  a structured way: n-linear entries cancel; n²-quadratic
  entries double.
- *Shear creates a ±n-biasing mechanism* (Ch7). Off-diagonal
  shear, which mass itself can source, breaks the ±n
  degeneracy in dynamics. This is the project's closest
  contact with the real-world matter/antimatter question.

### 5. What remains open

Questions the project raised but did not settle:

- *The physical content of the sign of n.* Multiple readings
  remain consistent with the math (handedness, particle/
  antiparticle, label-without-content). Which is correct
  depends on richer-structure questions (interactions, charge,
  cosmology) outside this project's scope.
- *Whether the wave-realist reading of φ is supportable as
  physics.* The project's mode picture suggests a real wave
  underlying mass; whether this can be reconciled with quantum
  measurement theory is a project of its own.
- *Whether the minimal manifold is the right abstraction.* The
  project worked in 1+1+1D; the real world has more extended
  dimensions and likely more compact structure. Whether the
  results generalize cleanly is a separate question.
- *The mechanism for spacetime bending.* This project applied
  Einstein's equations as a postulate. Whether the GRID
  framework's entropic-gravity pathway gives a derivation
  consistent with our setup is an open program.
- *The dynamics of off-diagonal shear.* If γ has its own
  equation of motion (rather than being a fixed parameter),
  the cosmological story of how shear evolves over time
  becomes a follow-on question.
- *Charge.* The project deferred charge entirely. A follow-up
  project introducing a second compact dimension w would take
  this up.

### 6. What this project leaves to follow-up projects

Three follow-up directions, in increasing order of scope:

- *A charge project.* Add a second compact dimension w; redo
  the mode-derivation; check whether charge emerges from
  compact momentum analogously to how mass emerged here. Open
  question: how the framing of "compact momentum" as charge
  vs. mass interacts when both compact directions are present.
- *A cosmological/dynamical project.* Promote γ from parameter
  to dynamical field; ask how shear evolves; investigate
  whether realistic baryon-asymmetry magnitudes can emerge.
- *A unification project.* Bring in the GRID framework's
  causal-network and entropy pathways as derivations of the
  postulates this project assumed. Check whether the project's
  structural results survive when the postulates are
  derivable.

### 7. Reflections on framing

A few framing observations worth recording for follow-up
work:

- *Compact-direction momentum can be read as either mass or
  charge.* Standard KK reads it as charge; this project read
  it as mass. The math is the same; the interpretation is a
  framing choice. Whether the choice has empirical content,
  or is purely a relabeling, is unsettled.
- *±n is a handedness in any framing.* Whether this handedness
  becomes observable depends on what other structure is
  present. In the diagonal-metric, isolated-mode setting, it
  does not. With shear, it does.
- *Mass is geometric in this framework.* Rest mass has no
  parameter beyond the compact circumference R_u and the
  integer n. The framework's geometry alone determines the
  mass spectrum; the project did not need to introduce a mass
  scale beyond R_u.

### 8. End of project

A brief sign-off: what has been established, what has been
left open, where to look next. Final statement of the
project's narrow claim:

> *Starting from light alone on a minimal manifold with one
> compact extra dimension, a discrete mass spectrum emerges,
> behaves inertially, sources gravitational bending, and
> exhibits a ±n handedness that becomes observable only in
> the presence of off-diagonal metric structure. Whether this
> picture corresponds to physics in the real world is a
> question follow-up projects can take up.*
