# Bounding mechanisms in GRID

Two structural facts hold in the GRID/MaSt architecture:

1. **Per-cell state spaces are bounded.**  This is what gives
   the lattice finite per-cell entropy, makes the holographic
   bound (A5) well-defined, and underwrites Jacobson-style
   gravity.
2. **The bound is implemented as a *wrap* (S¹ topology), not a
   *saturation* (interval topology).**  This is what gives
   U(1) gauge structure, conserved winding numbers, and
   ultimately charge quantization.

These are two separate properties of the same axiom A3.
Boundedness is what makes the lattice entropic; the *form* of
the bound determines whether electromagnetism also follows.

The same compact phase A3 also plays two distinct roles at two
**scales** — local (single cell) and global (sheets of many
cells).  Both involve "phase passing 2π and wrapping," but the
**information consequences are opposite** at the two scales,
and that opposition is what allows the same axiom to deliver
both gravity and charge quantization at once.

This document:

- Makes the two-scale picture (local vs global) explicit and
  links A3, A5, gravity, and charge quantization.
- Distinguishes *boundedness* from the specific *bounding
  mechanism* (wrap vs saturation), and shows that gravity
  depends only on boundedness while charge quantization
  requires the wrap topology specifically.

| Scale | Object | Wraparound is | Information consequence | Physical consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GRID** (Planck, ~1 L_P) | Single cell's phase θ on its ℵ-line | A *local* event: phase passes 2π at one cell | Carry **discarded** (only θ mod 2π is stored) | **Entropy generation, irreversibility, gravity** |
| **MaSt** (compactified, L_compact ≫ L_P) | A field configuration on a closed sheet (e.g. 2-torus) | A *global* topological feature: total winding number around a cycle | Winding **preserved** (homotopy invariant, can't change continuously) | **Charge quantization, topological selection rules** |

Same A3, opposite information treatment, opposite physical
output.

## The local wraparound (GRID scale): why the lattice is entropic

At the GRID scale, each lattice edge has its own phase θ on its
ℵ-line.  Phase fluctuates back and forth on each clock tick (A2)
and can accumulate either direction.  When it crosses the 2π
boundary, the cell's state register stores only `θ mod 2π` — the
integer winding count is **not held anywhere in the lattice**.

This is the structural difference between a clock dial and a
spring:

- **A spring** stores its full displacement x ∈ ℝ.  At thermal
  equilibrium kT, RMS displacement σ_x = √(kT/k_spring) is
  unbounded as T → ∞.  All trajectory information is in
  principle recoverable from (x, ẋ): Hamiltonian flow is
  reversible, Liouville's theorem preserves phase-space volume,
  no entropy is generated by the dynamics itself.
- **A clock dial** stores only `θ mod 2π`.  Two trajectories
  starting at different points on ℝ but reaching the same point
  on S¹ become indistinguishable after wrap.  The dynamics
  effectively projects ℝ → S¹, and that projection is **not
  invertible**.  Information is lost; entropy increases.

### Why information loss matters

For a closed Hamiltonian system, entropy is not generated by
internal dynamics — it can only be redistributed.  This is the
classical mechanics no-go: pure conservative dynamics doesn't
deliver thermodynamics.

For Jacobson's argument that gravity is thermodynamic, the
lattice must:

1. Have a finite entropy bound per region (holographic principle)
2. Generate entropy when energy crosses a horizon (Clausius)
3. Have a well-defined temperature at the horizon (Unruh)

A spring lattice cannot do (2): its dynamics is reversible.
A clock-dial lattice **can** do (2) because its phase space
naturally projects out the carry, generating entropy as initial
conditions wash out.  This local lossiness is what makes
Jacobson's mechanism applicable.

### Connection to A5 (entropy bound)

A5 (ζ = 1/4 bit per cell) is well-defined only because per-cell
information is **bounded**.  Phase on S¹ with resolution Δθ
stores at most log₂(2π/Δθ) bits — a topological bound, not a
thermal one.  A spring lattice with continuous unbounded ℝ-valued
DOFs would have unbounded information capacity per cell, and A5
would either be ill-defined or would violate the area-vs-volume
scaling of the holographic principle.

So **A5 requires A3's compactness** — the bounded state space is
prerequisite for the holographic bound.  This connection is now
documented explicitly in foundations.md A5.

### Connection to "the lattice cools down"

Cooling = irreversibility = ability to dump entropy.

A reversible system can't cool; it can only redistribute.  A
clock-dial lattice cools because the projection ℝ → S¹ is
irreversible — trajectory information is genuinely lost on each
wrap.  This is Landauer's principle in operation: discarding a
bit of carry information costs (and dumps) heat kT·ln(2).

The "entropic system" framing the agent invoked when explaining
why sim-gravity-2 succeeds where sim-gravity failed comes down
to exactly this: **the lattice is entropic because its state
space is compact and the dynamics discards the carry.**

## The global wraparound (MaSt scale): why charge is quantized

At the MaSt scale, many grid cells are arranged into closed
surfaces.  In the simplest case relevant to electromagnetism,
this is a 2-torus T² compactified from a (much larger) patch of
the grid.  A field configuration φ: T² → S¹ (assigning a phase
to each point of the torus) can be classified by its **winding
numbers** around the two cycles of the torus:

<!-- (n_1, n_2) ∈ π_1(S^1) × π_1(S^1) = ℤ × ℤ -->
$$
(n_1, n_2) \in \pi_1(S^1) \times \pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}
$$

These integers are **homotopy invariants**: a continuous
deformation of the field cannot change them.  They are
preserved by smooth dynamics, even when individual cells'
phases wrap around 2π locally — because the local wraps cancel
out around the closed cycle, leaving the global winding fixed.

### Why the carry is preserved here but lost at the GRID scale

This may seem paradoxical: didn't we just say the lattice
discards the carry?  The answer is **scope**:

- At the GRID scale, *one cell's* carry is discarded.  Local
  wraps at individual cells are irretrievable.
- At the MaSt scale, the **sum** of carries around a closed
  loop (the winding number) is a topological invariant.  Even
  though each individual cell's carry is locally lost, the
  global tally around a cycle is fixed by the field
  configuration's homotopy class.

It's the same as: in a digital register chain, a single bit's
overflow at one stage can be lost, but the *total* number of
overflows around a closed circuit is determined by the input
modulo the register size — a global invariant even when local
overflows are discarded.

### How this gives charge quantization

In MaSt, a charged particle is a standing-wave mode wrapping
around a compact dimension.  The winding number of that mode
*is* the particle's charge (in appropriate units).  Because
the winding is topologically conserved:

- Charge can only change by integer steps (winding can change
  only by ±1 under topology-changing processes — vortex
  nucleation / annihilation)
- A field with winding n carries charge ne (e set by the
  coupling constant, which is determined by α via A6)
- Charge is conserved continuously by the equations of motion
  (∂_μ J^μ = 0 follows from antisymmetry of F^μν in maxwell.md)

This is the standard picture of charge quantization from
topological field theory, mapped onto the GRID/MaSt
two-scale architecture.

## Why the two scales coexist without contradiction

A natural worry: if the lattice discards the carry locally, how
can the global winding be preserved?

The answer is in the topology.  Local wrap discarding is a
projection ℝ → S¹ at *each cell*.  But the *configuration* of
phases across many cells, viewed as a function from the
underlying torus to S¹, is what carries the winding number.
The winding is a global property of the function, not a local
property of any single cell.

You can think of it as the difference between:

- The clock on your wall (a single dial that wraps; you can't
  tell from its current reading how many full days have elapsed)
- The number of times you've walked around the block (a count
  that's preserved by your trajectory, even though no individual
  step records it)

The grid cells are like clock dials — each loses its own carry.
The closed sheet built from many cells is like the count of
laps around the block — it's preserved because it's a property
of the *whole walk*, not any single step.

## Implications for the architecture

This two-scale picture sharpens several existing GRID claims:

1. **A3's two roles**: previously, A3 was credited with both
   "U(1) gauge structure → EM" and "topological carry → charge
   quantization."  The new picture says: A3's local U(1)
   structure (single-cell phase compactness) gives the
   *information loss / entropy / gravity* path, while A3 +
   compact-dimension structure (multi-cell wrapping into sheets)
   gives the *winding / charge quantization* path.

2. **A5's well-definedness** comes from A3's local compactness.
   They're not parallel axioms; A5 *requires* A3 to even be
   formulable.

3. **The spring vs string-register choice in sim-gravity-2** is
   not arbitrary.  Springs (continuous unbounded ℝ-valued DOFs)
   structurally cannot host the holographic bound or generate
   entropy via irreversible dynamics.  Clock-dial / phase
   registers can do both.

4. **Gravity (Jacobson) and electromagnetism (Maxwell)** are no
   longer parallel derivations from independent axioms — they
   share A3 as their common topological seed, with the two
   information consequences (loss vs preservation) mapping to
   the two physical sectors.

## What if the bound were saturation instead of a wrap?

Boundedness — finite information capacity per cell — is what
makes the lattice entropic and underwrites gravity.  But that
boundedness can in principle be implemented two different
ways:

| Mechanism | State space | Topology | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wrap (current GRID)** | θ ∈ [0, 2π) with θ + 2π ≡ θ | S¹ (circle) | none |
| **Saturation** | θ ∈ [θ_min, θ_max] with hard clipping at endpoints | [θ_min, θ_max] (interval) | two endpoints |

Both have bounded state space, so both *might* be expected to
deliver gravity.  But they differ in topology, which has
consequences for electromagnetism.  This section works out the
comparison and concludes that **saturation would still give
gravity but break electromagnetism**.

### Gravity: would still work

For gravity (Jacobson's mechanism), the requirements are:

1. **Bounded entropy per cell** (A5: ζ bits per cell).
   - Wrap on S¹ at resolution Δθ stores log₂(2π/Δθ) bits.
   - Saturation on [θ_min, θ_max] of length L stores
     log₂(L/Δθ) bits.
   - Both are bounded.  If L = 2π, the per-cell information
     capacity is *identical*.  ζ would be the same; G would
     be the same.
2. **Irreversibility / entropy generation.**
   - Wrap: the dynamics on S¹ is locally bijective (rotation
     has an inverse), but the *storage* discards the integer
     winding count.  Information loss is in the storage
     choice.
   - Saturation: the dynamics itself is non-bijective at the
     boundary (multiple inputs map to θ_max under clipping).
     Information loss is *intrinsic* to the dynamics.
   - Both produce the irreversibility Jacobson's argument
     requires.  Saturation is, if anything, *more* dissipative
     because each clipping event is a hard collapse, not just
     a chosen storage truncation.
3. **Causal horizons with Unruh temperature.**
   - These come from A1 (lattice geometry) and A2 (Lorentzian
     signature), independent of A3's specific form.  Both
     wrap and saturation respect A1 and A2.

So gravity would still work under saturation.  The same
holographic bound, the same Clausius relation, the same
Einstein equations would follow.  The strength of gravity
(through ζ and α) would be unchanged if L = 2π.

### Electromagnetism: would break

For electromagnetism, the structure is more delicate.  Two
key requirements both fail under saturation.

**(a) Charge quantization requires the wrap topology.**

Charge quantization in GRID/MaSt comes from the topological
classification of field configurations on closed compact
sheets:

<!-- φ: T² → S¹  has homotopy classes (n_1, n_2) ∈ ℤ × ℤ -->
$$
\varphi: T^2 \to S^1
\quad \text{has homotopy classes}\quad
(n_1, n_2) \in \pi_1(S^1) \times \pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}
$$

The integer winding numbers exist *because* the target space
S¹ has a hole — π₁(S¹) = ℤ.  The winding number counts how
many times the field winds around the circle as you traverse a
cycle of the torus, and it cannot change by smooth deformation.

Under saturation, the target space is the interval [θ_min,
θ_max], which is **contractible** — π₁([0,1]) = 0.  An interval
has no hole to wind around.  Any field configuration φ: T² →
[θ_min, θ_max] can be smoothly deformed to a constant.  **There
is no topological invariant to count.  Charge would not
quantize.**

This is the cleanest single-line consequence: charge
quantization requires *circular* boundedness, not just any
boundedness.

**(b) Gauge invariance breaks at the boundary.**

A4 (local gauge invariance) requires that the physics be
unchanged under arbitrary local relabeling θ(x) → θ(x) + χ(x).
Under wrap, this works for any χ because S¹ has no boundary —
shifting by χ just slides everything around the circle.

Under saturation, a relabeling χ that pushes any cell's phase
past θ_max would be clipped.  The full continuous symmetry
under all relabelings is gone — only those χ that don't push
any cell past the bound are exact symmetries.  This is no
longer a U(1) gauge symmetry.

Without full gauge invariance:
- The link variables A_μ (which compensate for non-uniform
  relabeling) become ill-defined or partial.
- The gauge field tensor F_μν loses its derivation.
- Maxwell's equations are not produced.

So saturation breaks not just charge quantization but the
entire route from A3 + A4 to electromagnetism.

### What this means for the axiomatic story

The user's structural reading is correct: **the wrap is doing
double duty.**  Boundedness alone (whatever shape) gives
gravity; the *circular* form of the boundedness is what
additionally gives electromagnetism.

This sharpens the role of A3 in the axiom hierarchy:

- *A3 — boundedness* is structurally prerequisite for A5 and
  gravity.  Any compact bounding mechanism would do.
- *A3 — circularity* (the choice of S¹ rather than an
  interval) is what makes A3 + A4 produce U(1) gauge
  structure and charge quantization.

The two pieces are conceptually separable, and a hypothetical
"saturation universe" would have gravity but no
electromagnetism.  The actual universe needs both, so A3 must
be S¹, not an interval.  This is not arbitrary — it's forced
by the dual demand for entropic gravity *and* quantized
charge.

### Why this matters

Being able to identify *which* feature of A3 produces *which*
physical sector clarifies the architecture:

- The agent's "entropic system" framing for gravity points at
  boundedness, not specifically at circularity.
- The user's question about "loss of carry information" points
  at the irreversibility, which boundedness (wrap or
  saturation) provides.
- Charge quantization points specifically at the S¹ topology
  — and *would not be reproduced* by other bounding
  mechanisms.

So if some future GRID variant ever needed to break charge
quantization (e.g., to model a sector without conserved
electric charge), saturation would be the natural way to do
it without sacrificing gravity.  Conversely, if a variant
needed gravity at a different ζ or G, modifying the *length*
of the bounded state space (without changing its topology)
would do it without disturbing electromagnetism.

This decoupling of the two physical sectors via the two
properties of A3 (boundedness vs circularity) is itself a
structural insight worth keeping in the GRID architecture.

## What this leaves open

- **Quantitative entropy production rate** at a horizon from
  carry-discarding dynamics: the existing gravity derivation
  uses Clausius + holographic bound directly (Jacobson's
  argument).  A microscopic derivation of dS/dt from the rate
  of phase wraps in the lattice would close the loop but is
  not currently in the GRID files.
- **The role of higher modes on the ℵ-line**: A3 assigns the
  lowest mode (just θ) to charge / gauge structure.  Higher
  modes on the same compact ℵ-line carry mass and other
  internal quantum numbers (per foundations.md "ℵ-line"
  table).  Whether *those* modes have wraparound dynamics with
  the same entropic role, or behave differently, is unsettled.
- **Non-abelian generalization**: the two-scale picture given
  here is U(1) (single-phase circle) on each cell.  SU(2) and
  SU(3) generalizations would have richer local state spaces
  — the entropy / quantization story may need adjustment for
  non-abelian compact groups.
